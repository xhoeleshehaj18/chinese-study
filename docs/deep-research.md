# Deep research: getting from this app to speaking Mandarin

A second, deeper pass on the question in [`speaking-research.md`](speaking-research.md): what actually gets an adult English speaker speaking Mandarin, and what this app should do about it. It covers more ground and more recent work, including three 2025 meta-analyses, and adds two simulations of the app's own settings. September 2026.

**How to read the evidence labels:** **Strong** = several meta-analyses or many studies agree. **Moderate** = one meta-analysis, or a few controlled studies. **Early** = single studies, small samples, short trials, or industry reports.

Many journal pages were blocked from this environment. Where a number comes from a paper's abstract or a published summary rather than the full text, the source link is to the abstract.

---

## The short version

1. **Apps on their own produce small speaking gains** (Moderate). About 12 hours of Babbel moved learners 0.7 of an ACTFL sub-level, and only 59% improved at all. About 24 hours of Busuu got learners to Novice Mid: "isolated words and memorised phrases". Adding **interaction** is the biggest lever the research knows of: d ≈ 1.09 in a meta-analysis of 28 studies. → The app needs real turn-taking: role-play, an AI partner, and a path to people.
2. **Listening practice barely turns into speaking** (Strong). Across 31 studies, perception-only tone and sound training raised production by about 10% on trained items and 4.5% on new ones, with no reliable retention. → Speaking has to be practised as speaking, every day, from the start.
3. **The lasting tone problem is tones *inside words you know*, not tones in isolation** (Strong). Learners get good at isolated syllables quickly. Even advanced learners stay "fuzzy" about which tone belongs to which word, and struggle with tones in running speech. → Check tone recall on every Speak card. Keep tone practice going in words and phrases, not only in drills.
4. **Grading yourself is unreliable** (Moderate). People using flashcards alone overestimated what they'd remember by about 20%. → Make grading objective wherever the app can: tones drawn from memory, speech recognition, response time.
5. **Spacing matters more than volume** (Strong). Spaced practice has large effects on delayed tests. For sound training specifically, sessions a week apart kept 24% of the gain after four weeks, against 7% for sessions a day apart. → A drill passed in one long sitting should be confirmed on a later day and revisited on a schedule.
6. **Saying a word from memory is the skill that fades fastest** (Strong). With flashcards, recall of the *meaning* fell from 60% to 39% after a delay, and recall of the *word itself* fell from 59% to 25%. → The Speak cards are the right priority. They are also the cards most worth checking objectively.
7. **Fluency is speed, and speed comes from speeded practice and repetition** (Moderate). Native Mandarin runs at about 6–7 syllables a second. Repeating the same task mostly improves accuracy and complexity, and fluency less. Retelling under shrinking time limits targets speed directly. → Measure response time and speech rate, and build timed retelling.
8. **Being anxious hurts, and low-stakes practice helps** (Strong for the link, Early for the tools). Anxiety correlates r = −.36 with achievement. AI conversation partners and pinyin-first teaching both came with lower anxiety in their studies. → An always-available, judgement-free partner is worth building.
9. **The app will soon run out of material, not review time** (from the simulation below). With a unit test on a later day after each unit, the 283 phrases are all introduced in about 3½ months at best. Daily reviews then drop to about 10 cards. → Content growth and speaking activities are the bottleneck, not the scheduler.

## 1. What "speaking Chinese" takes

**Hours.** The US Foreign Service Institute puts Mandarin in its hardest group: about 88 weeks, or 2,200 class hours, to reach professional working proficiency (S-3). That's intensive full-time study ([FSI rankings](https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/blog/fsi-language-difficulty/)). A more realistic personal milestone is ACTFL **Intermediate Low**: you can "create with language" and handle a simple conversation about yourself and daily life. US college learners of Chinese reach about Novice Mid after two semesters and Intermediate Low after four ([ACTFL research brief](https://www.actfl.org/research/research-briefs/proficiency-levels-for-k16)). That is roughly 250–300 class hours plus homework.

**What apps alone achieve** (Moderate, with most studies on Spanish and French):
- **Babbel:** 54 learners studied Spanish for about 12 hours over 12 weeks. Their ACTFL OPIc score rose 0.7 sub-levels. 59% improved at least one sub-level, rising to 75% among those who studied 15+ hours. Study time was the strongest predictor, followed by motivation ([Loewen, Isbell & Sporn 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/flan.12454)).
- **Busuu:** after about 24 hours, learners went from Novice Low to Novice Mid (Vesselinov & Grego 2016, as reanalysed in the [Duolingo speaking report](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/duolingo-speaking-whitepaper.pdf)).
- **Rosetta Stone and Duolingo:** Rosetta Stone-only learners struggled in conversation compared with classroom learners. Duolingo Turkish learners did worse on oral tasks than on vocabulary and grammar (Lord 2015; Loewen et al. 2019; both summarised in the same report).
- **Duolingo's own report:** learners who completed the beginner units reached about A2 on the Pearson Versant speaking test. There was no control group, and it's the company's own study ([Jiang et al. 2021](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/duolingo-speaking-whitepaper.pdf)).

**For the app:** set a concrete goal and measure it. A good first target is **"talk for 5 minutes about yourself and your day, and understand simple questions back"**, which is roughly Intermediate Low. Everything below is judged by whether it moves you towards that.

## 2. Pronunciation: what matters for being understood

### Tones versus sounds
- For **native listeners in quiet**, Mandarin sentences with the tones flattened out stay largely understandable, because context fills in. In **noise**, flat tones make them much harder to follow ([JASA 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23862913/); Moderate).
- A 2025 study swapped the consonant, vowel or tone of one number in a spoken sequence. Native listeners recovered the intended number best when only the **consonant** was wrong, so vowel and tone errors were harder to see past ([PubMed 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40844136/); Early).
- Learners' tones are **worst in running speech**. Advanced learners identify isolated tones almost perfectly, yet struggle to reject a word said with the wrong tone, in isolation or in a sentence ([SSLA, advanced learners](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/advanced-second-language-learners-perception-of-lexical-tone-contrasts/BA813543C79288DF6B92E929442CB4BC); [Pelzl et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2021.689423/full); Strong).
- The most common confusion is **tone 2 with tone 3**, and it persists into advanced levels ([Pelzl 2019 review](https://benjamins.com/catalog/csl.18009.pel); [tones in loud speech, 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12383229/); Strong).

**For the app:**
- Tones matter, but mostly as part of words in context. Vowels and initial consonants matter as much.
- The "Hear tones in phrases" stage is exactly the right idea and deserves more of the time.
- The tone-2 vs tone-3 contrast deserves its own drill, like the tricky-sounds stage has.

### Training tone perception
- **High-variability phonetic training (HVPT)** works. Across 79 studies it gave g = 0.92 from pre-test to post-test and g = 0.67 against control groups, with long-term retention and some carry-over to new items. The number of talkers is one of the moderators ([Uchihara, Karas & Thomson 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/high-variability-phonetic-training-hvpt-a-metaanalysis-of-l2-perceptual-training-studies/6ABB8C1F32D88D53EA8D05A4565E76F6); Strong).
- A network meta-analysis ranked **six talkers** best ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2024_JSLHR-24-00599)). The app now has six voices for the sound drills, once their clips are generated.
- **Doses in the studies are small.** Classic studies used 8 sessions of about 15 minutes (Thomson 2011, via [Thomson's review](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/jslp.17038.tho)). A 2025 study got over 30% immediate gains from **45 minutes in total**. Spacing those sessions **7 days apart** kept 24% of the gain at 28 days, against 7% for 1 day apart. Longer gaps also levelled out differences in hearing ability ([Li, MLJ 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/modl.70081); Moderate).
- **Training with varied surrounding tones helps** learners handle tones in context ([Chang & Bowles 2015](https://sites.bu.edu/pamlab/files/2021/10/ChangBowles2015_JASA.pdf); Moderate).

**For the app:** the sound stages drill each skill until "49 of the last 50", which can be reached in one long sitting. The research says the *durable* part of the learning comes from spacing. Two changes follow:
- **Confirm a pass on a later day:** a 20-question check at 90%+ the next day or later. This mirrors how unit tests already work.
- **After passing, bring each sound drill back on a widening schedule,** for example one round a week, then fortnightly. The daily warm-up already covers part of this.

### From hearing tones to saying them
- Perception training transfers to production only a little. Across 31 studies, production went up **10.5% on trained items and 4.5% on untrained ones**, with no strong evidence it lasts. Perception and production gains barely correlated at the level of individual learners ([Applied Psycholinguistics meta-analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/does-perceptual-high-variability-phonetic-training-improve-l2-speech-production-a-metaanalysis-of-perceptionproduction-connection/E38D8F5CE65DC708137B0E95F97C6BC7); Strong).
- The best single case is Wang et al. (2003): perception training improved production by 18% ([JASA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12597196/)). But in a head-to-head test, perception practice improved only perception, and production practice improved only production ([Li & DeKeyser 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/perception-practice-production-practice-and-musical-ability-in-l2-mandarin-toneword-learning/EBCB48B5D485BDB8AA9AEA20FA3AB558)).
- **Feedback on your own voice works.** Beginners who saw their tones in Praat next to corrected versions made significantly larger gains than those given feedback by a teacher ([CALL 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09588221.2022.2037652); Moderate). Self-directed training with real-time tone feedback improved sentence-level tones over 2 weeks, while listen-and-repeat practice didn't ([IJCHL 2025](https://benjamins.com/catalog/ijchl.00040.wu); Early).
- **Speech recognition helps pronunciation** (g = 0.69 over 15 studies). It works best with explicit corrective feedback and over longer periods. Its effect on individual sounds is large, but **small on tones and intonation**. Practising alone with it had a small effect ([Ngo, Chen & Lai 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/effectiveness-of-automatic-speech-recognition-in-eslefl-pronunciation-a-metaanalysis/A915444CF252B61D14961D2FE733822D); Moderate).
- **Teaching tone 3 as a *low* tone** improved it in sentences and carried over to new words, better than teaching it as a dip-and-rise. The dip-and-rise method did better for isolated words ([He et al., Chinese as a Second Language](https://doi.org/10.1075/csl.51.3.02he); Early).

**For the app:**
- Speaking needs its own daily practice, with feedback on the learner's own voice. The pitch picture is the right tool.
- Speech recognition should stay a word check, not a tone check, which the app already says honestly.
- Tone 3 should be described first as "low", with the dip as the special case before a pause. The phrase pad already accepts "anything low". The "Say the four tones" tip still leads with "dip low, then come back up".

### Writing and colour
- **Tone marks help** beginners attach tones to new words (ηp² = .18; [Showalter & Hayes-Harb 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0267658313480154)).
- There's **no good evidence either way on tone colours** ([Hacking Chinese review](https://www.hackingchinese.com/does-using-colour-to-represent-mandarin-tones-make-them-easier-to-learn/)). They're harmless.
- **Delaying characters doesn't hurt speaking.** Early characters improved reading, with no difference in oral interviews ([Knell 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/flan.12281)). Pinyin-first learners reported less anxiety, and a short pinyin-only start helped pronunciation ([Ye 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/flan.12049); [review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/289249266_Three_approaches_to_beginning_Chinese_instruction_and_their_effects_on_oral_development_and_character_recognition)).

**For the app:** pinyin-first with optional characters is well supported. Keep it.

## 3. Vocabulary and phrases

- **Deliberate study beats picking words up.** Flashcards and similar activities averaged about 60% of words learned on immediate meaning-recall tests. After a delay that fell to **39% for meaning recall and 25% for form recall**, i.e. producing the word yourself ([Webb, Yanagisawa & Uchihara 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/modl.12671); Strong).
- By contrast, **incidental learning** from listening or reading picks up only 9–18% of new words, 13–15% from listening ([Webb, Uchihara & Yanagisawa 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/how-effective-is-second-language-incidental-vocabulary-learning-a-metaanalysis/E38E3468FD2090B1FA3051051DE8E70C)). Hearing a word more often helps only moderately (r = .34; [Uchihara et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12343)). **Interactive** listening tasks teach more words than passive ones ([Vos et al. 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12296)).
- **Learning in the productive direction** (meaning → word) built more kinds of word knowledge than the receptive direction ([Webb 2009](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0033688209343854)).
- **Spacing has a large effect on delayed tests,** with no clear difference between equal and expanding gaps ([Kim & Webb 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12479); Strong).
- **Chunks carry fluency.** Stored multi-word phrases are processed faster by learners of Chinese at all levels ([Humanities & Social Sciences Communications 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-02137-4)), and Mandarin speakers pause around chunks ([PMC 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9978407/)).
- **Grading yourself is unreliable:** with flashcards alone, learners' predictions of their own recall were inflated by about 20% ([Metacognition & Learning 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-024-09406-w)).

**For the app:**
- The phrase-first, both-directions, FSRS design is well supported.
- The weak spot is the self-graded **Speak** card, which is the most valuable and fastest-fading kind. It should get an objective check: tones drawn from memory (first research pass, #2), a speech-recognition word match, and response time (#3).
- Listening is for comprehension and speed, not for learning new words, which should keep coming through the scheduled cards.

### Simulation: daily review load
The app's FSRS settings (retention 0.9, maximum interval 365 days) over a year with 283 phrases, 2 cards each. Recall on each review was drawn from FSRS's own prediction:

| New phrases a day | Retention | Card reviews a day, days 1–30 | Days 30–90 | Days 90–180 | Days 180–365 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 (app default, steady) | 0.90 | 50 | 47 | 12 | 9 |
| 5 | 0.85 | 44 | 40 | 10 | 9 |
| 10 | 0.90 | 99 | 28 | 10 | 9 |
| about 3 (the real pace, see below) | 0.90 | 30 | 46 | 18 | 9 |

At about 10 seconds a card, that is **5–9 minutes a day** while phrases are coming in, then about 2 minutes. Lowering retention to 0.85 saves only about 12%, so 0.9 is the right setting for a speaking goal. This matches the FSRS maintainers' guidance that the optimum is around 0.85–0.9 ([FSRS wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/wiki/The-optimal-retention)).

The real finding is how soon the phrases run out. Each unit's 11 or so phrases take about 3 days at 5 a day, and its test opens the day after. At best, that's about 104 days for all 26 units: roughly 3½ months, or about 3 new phrases a day on average. After that, the review load is tiny. Growing the content, towards the 500 words of the HSK 3.0 level 2 syllabus ([2025–26 syllabus](https://www.hanzistroke.com/blog/new-hsk-3-guide)), and adding speaking activities is what will fill the time usefully.

## 4. Grammar for speaking

- **Explicit teaching of grammar works,** and works better than implicit exposure for both simple and complex features. The effect on free speech is smaller than on controlled tests (0.45 vs 0.58; [Spada & Tomita 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00562.x); Strong).
- **Structural priming** means hearing a model sentence and then producing one with the same structure. It helped classroom learners of Mandarin use the dative correctly, with effects lasting beyond the session ([SSLA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/structural-priming-facilitates-l2-learning-of-the-dative-alternation-in-mandarin/900547B82D989C0E9D90CEC52C7CF7BF)). Priming is strongest when the prime and target share words ([meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/302553211_A_meta-analysis_of_syntactic_priming_in_language_production); Moderate).
- **Practice has to be spaced and repeated to become automatic.** Which learners gain most from spaced versus massed grammar practice depends on their aptitudes ([Suzuki & DeKeyser 2017](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Suzuki-DeKeyser-2017-AP.pdf)). Knowledge only becomes fast and effortless through practice in the target skill ([DeKeyser & Suzuki 2025](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/PreprintDeKeyser-R.-M.-Suzuki-Y.-2025.-Skill-acquisition-theory.-In-B.-VanPatten-G.-D.-Keating-S.-Wulff-Eds.-Theories-in-second-language-acquisition-An-introduction-4th-ed.-pp.-157-182-.pdf)).

**For the app:**
- The short grammar notes are the explicit part, and they're good.
- What's missing is practice using the patterns. The sentence-pattern drills (first pass, #6) should work as **primes**: hear 我想喝茶, then say 我想___ with a new word, with the pattern scheduled by FSRS.
- Start with the patterns English speakers most often get wrong, which the notes already flag:
  - 是 with adjectives;
  - time words and place phrases before the verb;
  - measure words;
  - 了 for completed actions, and 没 without 了;
  - 也 and 都 before the verb;
  - 比 with no 很.

## 5. Fluency

- Native Mandarin articulation runs at about **6.6 syllables a second** in spontaneous speech (one corpus study; [Speech Prosody 2024](https://www.isca-archive.org/speechprosody_2024/zhu24_speechprosody.pdf)). L2 speakers are reliably slower, and your speaking rate in English predicts your rate in the new language ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5848867/)).
- A short **pre-study-abroad fluency course** gave L2 Mandarin learners shorter planning time, faster speech and longer answers. The advantage lasted through 10 months abroad ([Wang & Halenko 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12694); Moderate).
- **Repeating a speaking task** mainly improves accuracy and complexity, with smaller fluency gains ([System 2025 meta-analysis](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0346251X25002787)).
- The **4/3/2** technique targets speed directly: tell the same thing in 4, then 3, then 2 minutes. One learner went from 86 to 127 words a minute ([Nation](https://ihworld.com/ih-journal/issues/issue-44/an-activity-for-oral-fluency-development/)).

**For the app:**
- Measure what fluency is: **response time** on Speak cards, plus **speech rate and pausing** in retellings. The pitch tracker already knows which moments are voiced.
- Build the timed "About me" retelling (first pass, #8), and show your syllables-per-second creeping towards 3–4.

## 6. Listening input

- Listening builds comprehension, and understanding the reply is half of any conversation. But it teaches new words slowly (see §3).
- Tones in natural sentences are harder than in words, for everyone learning ([Pelzl 2019](https://benjamins.com/catalog/csl.18009.pel)).

**For the app:**
- Dialogues at natural speed in two voices (first pass, #7) cover both needs. Use them for comprehension, then shadowing, then role-play.
- Keep them within known words, so the listening practises speed and tones rather than guessing vocabulary.

## 7. Conversation and feedback

- **Interaction** had large effects across 28 studies (d ≈ 1.09 for vocabulary and grammar), clearer on delayed tests ([Mackey & Goo 2007](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Interaction-research-in-SLA-:-A-meta-analysis-and-Mackey-Goo/2b270feebe31f2f01e7c645cc00f8e94d504fb8b); Strong).
- **Correction works, and prompts beat recasts.** Getting you to fix the mistake yourself works better than just saying the correct version back, and the effects last ([Lyster & Saito 2010](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ892626); Strong).
- **For Mandarin specifically:** 44 young learners randomly assigned to talk with a voice assistant improved listening and speaking, especially how easy they were to understand. They also held longer conversations than a peer-talk group, which didn't improve ([CALL 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09588221.2024.2317849); Early).
- **Large language models as partners:** a 4-week randomised trial with 83 EFL students reported better oral proficiency and less anxiety ([Zheng et al. 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09588221.2025.2453191)). Studies of voice chatbots also report better fluency and willingness to speak ([2025 mixed-methods study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05550-z)). All of this is Early: short, mostly English as the target language, and small samples.
- **Anxiety** correlates r = −.36 with achievement across 105 samples ([Teimouri, Goetze & Plonsky 2019](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/second-language-anxiety-and-achievement/B140169CF7C0BB6F5CE7BB0D1F9CDFA0)).

**For the app:**
- **Scripted role-play first** (offline, free): the app says one side of a dialogue and you say the other.
- **Then an optional AI partner** (first pass, #10). Keep it to words you know, correct with prompts before giving the answer, and turn gaps into new cards.
- **Then people:** missions to try with a tutor or language partner, and a "what I can say" page (first pass, #11).

## 8. Keeping going

- **Habits take a while:** a median of 66 days to become automatic, ranging from 18 to 254 ([Lally et al. 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
- **If–then plans** ("after breakfast, at the kitchen table, I'll do my session") had a d = .65 effect on reaching goals across 94 tests ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/37367696_Implementation_Intentions_and_Goal_Achievement_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Effects_and_Processes)).
- **Gamification** has small-to-moderate effects on motivation ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)). Streaks can turn into "don't break the chain" anxiety unless the reward is tied to actual competence ([PMC 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11163042/)).
- In the Babbel study, **study time and motivation** were what predicted speaking gains ([Loewen et al. 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/flan.12454)).

**For the app:**
- A one-time **plan** screen: when, where, and how many minutes. Remind at that time.
- Keep the streak, but add **competence milestones**: first 5-minute self-introduction, fastest response time, first role-play done without hints.
- A monthly **recording next to last month's** (first pass, #8) makes progress visible, which is the motivating part.

## What this changes in the roadmap

The first research pass is in [`speaking-research.md`](speaking-research.md). Of its items, #1 (several voices), #5 (daily shadowing) and part of #9 (more content) are done. The evidence above reorders the rest, and adds four new items (★):

| Priority | Change | Why (strongest evidence) | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Draw the tones on Speak cards** from memory, as an objective part of the rating | Fuzzy tone memories for words; self-grading inflated about 20%; pitch gestures help | Small |
| 2 | **Response time on Speak cards**, used in the rating and shown as a trend | Automatization; skill-specific practice | Small |
| 3 | ★ **Confirm sound-stage passes on a later day, then revisit them on a widening schedule** | Spacing in phonetic training: 24% vs 7% retained | Small |
| 4 | **Review log** (card, rating, time, response time, tone check) | Needed for 1–3, and for tuning FSRS to you | Tiny |
| 5 | **Dialogues with role-play** in two voices: listen → shadow → role A → role B | Interaction, task repetition, comprehension at speed | Medium |
| 6 | **Sentence patterns as priming drills** (hear a model, produce with a new word) | Explicit instruction plus structural priming | Medium |
| 7 | **"About me" script with 4/3/2 retelling and speech-rate tracking** | Fluency training; self-relevant content; visible progress | Medium |
| 8 | ★ **Tone 2 vs tone 3 contrast drill**, and describe tone 3 as low first | The most persistent confusion; low-tone teaching carries over to sentences | Small |
| 9 | **More content** towards about 500 words, by situation | The app runs out of new phrases in about 3½ months | Large |
| 10 | **Optional AI conversation partner** (your own API key, limited to known words, corrects with prompts) | Interaction; less anxiety (Early) | Medium |
| 11 | **Bridge to people:** missions and a "what I can say" page | Interaction with humans is still the best-evidenced route | Small |
| 12 | ★ **Plan screen** (when, where, minutes) and **competence milestones** alongside the streak | If–then plans; habit formation; streak anxiety | Small |
| 13 | ★ **A clear goal on the Me tab:** "5 minutes about yourself", with the monthly recording as the check | Proficiency milestones (ACTFL Intermediate Low) | Small |
| 14 | Better automatic tone scoring | Speech recognition helps little with tones; automated tone ratings reach about 80% | Medium–large |

**Settings, checked against the evidence:**
- **FSRS retention 0.9:** keep it. 0.85 saves only about 12% of reviews.
- **5 new phrases a day:** a sensible default, 5–9 minutes of reviews while phrases are coming in.
- **The 49-of-50 gates:** fine as a bar (a steady 90% passes in about 300 answers), but add the delayed confirmation (#3).
- **A daily shape the research supports**, about 25 minutes:
  - warm-up drills, 3 minutes;
  - reviews, 8–10 minutes;
  - shadowing, 3 minutes;
  - role-play or retelling, 5–10 minutes.

## What the research doesn't settle

- **AI conversation partners:** the studies are short and small, mostly with English as the target language, and none follows learners of Mandarin for months.
- **Tone intelligibility:** most of it is measured on native listeners hearing manipulated native speech, not learners' actual errors.
- **Hours to a given level:** the figures come from classrooms and government courses. Self-study with an app is less studied, and its results vary more between people.
